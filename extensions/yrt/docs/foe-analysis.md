# Foe Analysis: Delve & Ironsworn in a YRT Setting

YRT has no supernatural. Every creature must be explainable through manite biology (mana-exposed gestation, mana-processing organs, wild-mana environments), mundane biology, or ordinary humans. Souls, ghosts, spirits, divine magic, prophecy, time travel, and true undead do not exist in this setting. Black mana can animate dead tissue as a mindless construct, but it cannot restore personality or purpose.

_Totals across the 58 foes: 38 fit as-is, 5 fit with a rework or reframe, 15 don't fit._

## Patterns worth noting

The cleanest fits are mundane animals and humans — about half the list. No changes needed.

The next-cleanest fits are creatures whose oddities are already biological and just need a mana-biology label: carnivorous plants (Verdant), camouflagers (Gray), venomous hallucinogens (Amber), bioluminescent scales (Luminous), and oversized blight-gestated beasts (Azure for size, whatever colour the local blight is for the quirks).

The foes that fail all have the same underlying problem: they require a soul, spirit, or conscious will to persist after death, or they require genuine supernatural agency (primordials, circle of stones, tempest). YRT's Black mana can move dead meat but cannot think with it. Bonewalkers, Bog Rot, Bonehorde, Frostbound, Haunt, Hollow, Iron Revenant, Sodden, Thrall, and Wight all share this flaw.

Most of the no-fit foes have a clean rework: replace the "spirit-driven" element with an illegal construct puppeted by a living handler via Amber command-seed. It preserves the encounter and keeps the antagonist (the handler) in-world.

The Husk and the Mystic are both excellent YRT-native foes as written — they're the setting's own mana-addicted wizard and black-market Amber user respectively. The Iron-Wracked Beast also fits the setting flawlessly as a consequence of pre-Fall industrial mana contamination.
